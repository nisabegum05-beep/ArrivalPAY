# Market rationale and validation plan

ArrivalPay targets participating institutions collecting conditional enrollment deposits from students who need cross-border funding. The initial corridor is TRY to Stellar Testnet USDC. It is a product hypothesis, not a signed institutional rollout.

## Primary evidence

- [University of Sheffield fee-deposit guidance](https://www.sheffield.ac.uk/fees/fee-deposits) describes deposits before CAS issuance for applicable applicants. This establishes a specific workflow; it does not show that all international students pay deposits or that Sheffield endorses ArrivalPay.
- [Flywire's 2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1580560/000119312526067540/flyw-20251231.htm) reports more than $37.6 billion in total payment volume across its business. It is evidence of established payment demand and competition, not conditional-deposit volume.

The brief's roughly 6.9 million global student claim is omitted because it was not substantiated with a matching primary source during this release review. General student population also should not be equated with annual new deposit payers.

## Scenario, not a measured TAM

Assume 1.4–2.0 million new mobile students per year, a 20–40% deposit-paying share, and a $2,000–$5,000 modeled deposit. Multiplication produces $0.56–$4.0 billion of hypothetical annual payment flow. All three inputs are unvalidated assumptions from the brief. This is not ArrivalPay revenue, market share or an adoption forecast.

## Alternatives and intended differentiation

Institutions can use international payment providers, bank transfers and their own finance systems. ArrivalPay investigates whether explicit on-chain release/refund rules plus a reusable student wallet improve visibility and operational handling. It does not claim existing providers lack refund products. Banking access, compliance, reconciliation and support remain substantial requirements beyond a smart contract.

## First pilot questions

1. Will an institution agree to hold funds under explicit release/refund deadlines?
2. Can students complete Freighter onboarding, trustlines and funding without support?
3. Does a shared record reduce disputes or manual status inquiries?
4. Can a licensed Anchor provide reliable settlement and acceptable pricing in the corridor?
5. Do students reuse the wallet after the initial payment, including arrival services?

Measure onboarding completion, time to settled funding, support requests, settlement/refund completion and later wallet use. Pricing and revenue remain open hypotheses. No interviews, partnerships, traction or willingness-to-pay evidence have been supplied.
